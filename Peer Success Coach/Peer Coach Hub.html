Ground rules

  • Nothing sends or submits on its own. Emails are drafts and forms are filled in but not submitted. You review and click the final button.
  • Read only means read only. For the Coaching Appt Record, Claude looks but never edits, saves or copies its data.
  • "Not set" means hands off. Claude doesn't open a system until you set its access level here.
  • No student information on this page. Names, grades and meeting notes stay in their own systems and folders.
  • Record only with consent. Ask "I'm going to record this for my notes, is that okay?"

Meeting SOP

A 30-minute meeting, taken from how you ran your September meetings. Scope: executive functioning, meaning time management, organization, study habits and self-care. Academic tutoring and class selection get referred out.

Before

Prep

  • Read what they said they want to work on
  • Brain dump: likely causes and ideas
  • Pick 3–4 iceberg questions
  • Returning student: pull up last plan
0–3 min

Get to know them

  • Smile, introduce yourself
  • Where are you from? What brought you to Logan? Major?
  • Returning: "How did the plan go? What worked?"
3–5 min

Explain what we do

  • Talk and come up with ideas
  • Narrow to the one with the highest payoff, something to start today
  • Make a plan and an accountability plan
5–15 min

Iceberg

  • What does success look like?
  • What are you doing well already?
  • What's the biggest difficulty right now?
  • What changed? How are you spending your time?
15–27 min

Pick one and plan it

  • Choose one idea from the toolkit
  • Fill in the plan template below
  • Set when and how you'll check in
After

Follow through

  • Email recap: what we discussed, the plan, links
  • Log summary in the USU system and the Coaching Appt Record
  • Send the promised check-in message
  • Meet again in about 2 weeks

Coaching toolkit

Strategies you use

  • Office hours plan: make a success plan for each class, take it to the professor for feedback, ask about UTFs and TAs
  • Lock-in study routine with the Pomodoro technique and music
  • Body doubling: study alongside someone for accountability
  • Calendar setup: class schedule plus homework blocks in Google Calendar
  • Beat procrastination: "just start" and break work into smaller steps so success is guaranteed
  • Balance and fun: do something you love every day and put campus events on the calendar

Note-taking plan

  1. Before class: read key terms and the summary, and find answers to the chapter questions
  2. In class: use symbols. ★ means review later, ! means especially important, ? means needs more explanation
  3. After class: review with the textbook, fill in the ★ items and answer the ? items
